## The Scent Profile

### Top Notes (0-15 minutes)
- **Cardamom:** Spicy, warm opening
- **Iris:** Powdery, elegant
- **Violet:** Soft, slightly sweet

### Heart Notes (15 minutes - 2 hours)
- **Australian Sandalwood:** Creamy, woody core
- **Cedar:** Dry, aromatic
- **Leather:** Subtle, sophisticated

### Base Notes (2-8 hours)
- **Papyrus:** Earthy, slightly smoky
- **Ambrox:** Musky, animalic (synthetic ambergris)
- **Australian Sandalwood:** Lingers as the dominant note

### The Accord

Santal 33's genius lies in its **sandalwood-leather-cedar** accord. The sandalwood is creamy and rich, the leather is subtle (not overpowering), and the cedar adds dry warmth. The result is a fragrance that's both comforting and intriguing.

**Perfumer's note:** The sandalwood used is Australian (not Indian Mysore), which has a different character—more woody, less creamy. This gives Santal 33 its distinctive dry-down.

## Why Did Santal 33 Become So Ubiquitous?

Santal 33's success is no accident. It hits several cultural sweet spots:

1. **Unisex appeal:** Works on all genders, aligning with the gender-fluid trend
2. **Distinctive but not challenging:** Unique enough to be recognizable, accessible enough to be wearable
3. **Instagram era:** The minimalist bottle and "cool girl" association made it a status symbol
4. **Hotel collaboration:** Le Labo's partnership with Edition Hotels introduced it to a wider audience

**The downside:** Its ubiquity has made it less "niche" than it once was. If you're looking for something truly rare, Santal 33 may no longer fit the bill.
